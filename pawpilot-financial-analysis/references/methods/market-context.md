# Market evidence method

Use returned source receipts, timestamps and original links. Do not launch a second retrieval workflow to apply this method.

Separate confirmed events, attributed claims, expectations and social discussion. Compare publication time, event time and market observation time; a newly retrieved old article is not breaking news. Distinguish information plausibly priced in from a new catalyst, labeling that assessment as interpretation.

Map each material catalyst to its transmission channel: demand/supply, liquidity, positioning, funding, protocol/counterparty risk or broader risk appetite. Explain why it matters for the asset and the position's side and time horizon. Correlation alone does not prove causation. Assess attribution, original sourcing and corroboration; copied headlines do not count as independent confirmations.

Weigh supporting and contrary evidence, explain the balance and state observable conditions that would change the view. Give a useful conditional bias rather than a certain price target, invented probability or a bare 'decide yourself'. X discussions are sentiment samples with selection/manipulation risk, not representative polling. X events and X discussions have separate coverage. Unavailable, stale, failed and successful-zero-results are different observations; missing X does not become neutral sentiment.
