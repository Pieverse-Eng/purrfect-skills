# Strategy concepts

These are hypotheses about market behavior. Select an approach because its
assumptions fit the question and evidence, not because a label implies profit.
Every approach needs an identified instrument, observation interval, horizon,
price units and realistic costs. Parameter choices below are conceptual choices,
not platform defaults.

## Trend following

A trend approach seeks continuation of a directional move. Price structure and
moving-average slopes can describe direction at a chosen horizon. Lag may help
ignore small fluctuations but also delays recognition of a reversal. Sideways
markets can produce repeated losing crossings. A rising average establishes a
property of past prices, not a future return or the reason for the move. Compare
observations on the same series and interval; a short-term decline can coexist
with a longer-term rise. [Fidelity: moving averages](https://www.fidelity.com/viewpoints/active-investor/moving-averages).

## Breakouts and pullbacks

A breakout hypothesis asks whether price moving beyond a previously defined
range reflects a durable change in supply and demand. Define the range from
observations available before the event; redrawing it after seeing the outcome
hides failures. A close beyond a boundary and subsequent behavior give different
evidence from a brief intrabar excursion. False breaks and gaps can defeat a
seemingly clear pattern. [Fidelity: chart patterns](https://www.fidelity.com/bin-public/060_www_fidelity_com/documents/learning-center/Idenitfying-Chart-Patterns.pdf).

A pullback hypothesis treats a counter-move as temporary within a broader trend.
It can improve the entry price relative to chasing a move, but a supposed
pullback may be the beginning of a reversal or may never occur. Specify which
observations would contradict continuation, rather than assuming a former price
level will hold. These ideas require enough timestamped history to identify the
prior move and the counter-move. [Fidelity: trading with the trend](https://www.fidelity.com/bin-public/060_www_fidelity_com/documents/learning-center/Transcript_Trade-with-the-trend_v2.pdf).

## Mean reversion and ranges

Mean reversion hypothesizes that a deviation from a specified reference will
shrink. State what the reference represents, its estimation window and why it
might remain relevant. A changing business or market regime can move the
reference itself; distance from an average does not prove mispricing.
[Fidelity: mean reversion concept](https://research2.fidelity.com/fidelity/research/reports/release2/resources/downloads/ETF_GUIDE_Fidelity.pdf).

Range trading is a more local hypothesis that previously observed boundaries
will continue to contain price. Repeated turns can make a boundary useful to
study, but cannot guarantee another turn. Breakouts, transaction costs and thin
depth can invalidate the apparent opportunity. Boundaries are zones estimated
from a particular history, not exact future execution prices.
[Fidelity: support and resistance](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/trading-investing/technical-analysis/support-and-resistance?print=true-0).

## Event-driven hypotheses

An event matters through its economic effect and how it differs from prior
expectations. A favorable announcement can coexist with a falling price. Follow
material company claims to filings and distinguish management discussion from
financial statements; inspect business exposure and risks rather than matching
only a headline to a ticker. [SEC: reading a 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/answers/reada10k.htm).

As an analytical application, trace the change through volumes, prices, costs,
contracts and financing to the chosen instrument. Compare direct exposure,
indirect exposure and waiting without assuming an indirect expression is better.
A catalyst's timing must fit the instrument's expiry and holding period. Missing
expectations, uncertain pass-through or a weak transmission link can justify a
conditional view or no action. Thesis invalidation is an economic condition;
a price stop is an execution/risk choice and does not prove the thesis false.
